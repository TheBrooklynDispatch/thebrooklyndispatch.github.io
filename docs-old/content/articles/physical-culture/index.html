Up until recently, I didn't want to be materialistic. I didn't see the point in buying things I could access digitally. Books, films, music. Everything was available instantly, cheaply and almost invisibly.

Over time, though, I started to think differently. Objects aren't meaningful because they're expensive or rare. They're meaningful because of what they allow us to do.

Convenience makes culture easier to consume. It does not always make it easier to remember.

Objects create rituals

A record asks you to choose a side and turn it over. A book waits on a shelf after you finish it. A cinema ticket becomes evidence that you were somewhere, at a particular hour, with other people.

These are small frictions. That is part of their value.

Sharing is part of ownership

By owning an item, you also own the ability to share it. Lending a favorite record to a friend or passing on a book that changed you gives the object another life.
