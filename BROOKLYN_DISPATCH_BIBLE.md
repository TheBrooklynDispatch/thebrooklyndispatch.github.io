# The Brooklyn Dispatch Bible

> Build something worth returning to.

---

# Mission

The Brooklyn Dispatch exists to help readers discover culture worth spending time with.

It is an independent publication about film, books, New York and the places they lead.

The goal is not to tell readers what to think.

The goal is to help them discover what to experience next.

---

# Perspective

Brooklyn is our point of view.

It is not our audience.

The publication should feel just as relevant to someone in London, Melbourne or Chicago as it does to someone living in Williamsburg.

---

# Promise

Film, books and the places they lead.

---

# The Reader

Our reader is curious.

They enjoy independent cinema, bookstores, records and cities.

They value recommendations from people whose taste they trust.

They would rather read one excellent piece than twenty forgettable ones.

The website should reward curiosity.

---

# Editorial Principles

## Curiosity over certainty.

Leave room for ambiguity.

---

## Depth over volume.

Publish because something is worth saying, not because a schedule demands it.

---

## Taste is useful.

Every article should leave the reader with somewhere worth going next.

A film.

A book.

A place.

An essay.

A conversation.

---

## Respect attention.

Attention is the reader's most valuable resource.

Earn it.

---

## Brooklyn is a perspective.

Not a limitation.

---

# Design Principles

The website should feel calm.

It should feel like walking into an independent bookstore.

White space is a feature.

Typography is the design.

Nothing moves without a reason.

The writing is always more important than the interface.

---

# Homepage Philosophy

The homepage is not a news feed.

It is the front window of the publication.

One outstanding story is better than ten average ones.

Readers should immediately understand what the publication values.

---

# Article Philosophy

Articles should feel timeless.

Good criticism should still be worth reading years later.

Every article should end by helping the reader continue exploring.

---

# Reviews

A review is not a verdict.

It is an invitation to think more deeply about a work.

Avoid scores.

Avoid stars.

Write conclusions, not ratings.

---

# Collections

Collections should become more valuable over time.

The publication should become a library rather than an archive.

---

# Things We Never Do

We never chase clicks.

We never publish because everyone else is.

We never mistake confidence for certainty.

We never waste the reader's time.

---

# The Question

Whenever we build something new, ask:

Does this make The Brooklyn Dispatch a place worth returning to?
