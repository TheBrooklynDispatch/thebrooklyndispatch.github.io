Cannes - 3 Months On

Three months after Cannes, the films that dominated the Croisette are beginning to find their place in the U.S. film landscape.

By Steve Berkowitz August 9, 2026

It has been three months since Cannes. For most of us, that means three months of waiting.

Many of the films that generated the most excitement in May still haven't been released in the U.S. In the meantime, attention has naturally moved elsewhere. Summer has belonged to blockbuster cinema, from The Odyssey to Spider-Man: Brand New Day. The films of Cannes haven't disappeared, but they aren't quite as prevalent in the audience's mind as they were three months ago.

That is beginning to change.

The New York Film Festival has announced its Main Slate, bringing a significant group of Cannes films to New York this fall. Distributors have continued to acquire films and, more importantly, put release dates against them. Studios are also beginning to settle their fall calendars and decide which films and performances will receive serious Oscar pushes.

Three months also gives us some useful distance from the festival itself. Cannes is an unusual environment in which to judge the future of a film. Reviews appear almost immediately after a premiere. Awards speculation follows. A film can become one of the most discussed movies in the world before almost anyone outside Cannes has seen it.

That three-month lag isn't just an annoyance; it’s a necessary buffer. Cannes is a pressure cooker. When you’re running on three hours of sleep and espresso, sitting in the Lumière, it's easy to mistake a standing ovation for a masterpiece. August gives us perspective. It lets the hype evaporate so we can ask the pragmatic questions that actually matter for American audiences: Who bought what? Who got left stranded? And which films are actually positioned to make noise this winter?

I tracked down all 41 titles across Cannes’s Official Competition and Un Certain Regard selections, along with the major winners from Critics' Week and Directors' Fortnight. Here is where they stand three months on.

Reporting note: U.S. distribution and release information is current as of August 10, 2026. "No U.S. distributor announced" means that I could find no publicly announced U.S. acquisition in distributor announcements or major trade reporting. International sales agents and distributors in other countries are not counted as U.S. distribution.

Elsewhere at Cannes

Competition and Un Certain Regard aren't the entirety of Cannes. Critics' Week and Directors' Fortnight run alongside the Official Selection and have long been places to find films that can be easy to miss amid the attention given to the Palme d'Or race.

Two winners in particular are worth keeping on the list.

Three Months On

Look at James Gray’s Paper Tiger. It walked out of France completely empty-handed, yet between NEON’s backing, a stacked cast, a November slot, and NYFF’s Opening Night, it has a vastly clearer runway to the Oscars than half the official prize winners. Look at Jordan Firstman’s Club Kid, which turned an unheralded side-section slot into a $17 million A24 deal.

Meanwhile, heavyweights like Farhadi, Nemes, and Ira Sachs are sitting without U.S. dates. That isn't a strike against their quality, but it reflects a cautious, risk-averse domestic market.

NYFF is our first real reality check. With eleven titles from this list heading to Lincoln Center, New York audiences will finally get to separate genuine contenders from festival hall mirages. Expect this table to look wildly different by the time Gotham and Spirit Award nominations land in November.
