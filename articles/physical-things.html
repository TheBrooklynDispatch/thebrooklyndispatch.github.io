Why Physical Things Still Matter

In a world built around convenience, objects can help us slow down, remember, and share what we value.

By Steve Berkowitz · August 6, 2026 · 5 min read

Up until recently, I did not want to be materialistic. I did not see the point in buying things I could access digitally. Books, films, and music were all available instantly through Kindle, streaming, and Spotify.

Over the last year, I have started to think differently. Objects are not meaningful because they are expensive or rare. They are meaningful because of what they allow us to do. When those objects contain art, the physical can take on a new significance.

We live in a world built around convenience. Music arrives instantly. Films disappear from streaming services without warning. Furniture is designed to be replaced rather than repaired. It has never been easier to consume something, but it has also never been easier to forget it.

The return to physical media offers a small correction. It encourages us to slow down, commit to the things we value, and engage with art more intentionally. A record asks you to choose a side. A book gathers evidence of where it has been. A film on a shelf waits to be shared.

By owning an item, you also own the ability to pass it along. Much of its meaning comes from that possibility: lending a favorite record to a friend, giving away a book that changed you, or leaving an object in someone else's care for the next chapter of its life.
